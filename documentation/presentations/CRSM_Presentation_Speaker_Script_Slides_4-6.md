# CRSM Presentation Speaker Script

## Slide 4 — Customer repair intake

“This slide shows the first step in the customer journey: submitting a repair request.

The customer records the device type, brand, model, specifications, serial number, reported problem, and requested service. Capturing these details at intake gives the technician a consistent starting point and reduces back-and-forth questions later.

The customer also selects a drop-off appointment and can attach up to three readable PNG or JPEG photos. Each photo is limited to 2 MB. These images help staff understand visible damage before the device arrives and give the repair record useful evidence throughout the workflow.

The important point is that intake combines structured device information with the customer’s own description and supporting photos. That creates a clearer handoff from the customer to the service team.”

Transition: “Once the request is submitted, the system moves it through a controlled repair lifecycle.”

## Slide 5 — Repair lifecycle

“The repair lifecycle has four connected stages.

First, the customer submits the request and an Admin assigns an eligible technician. Second, the technician diagnoses the issue and prepares an estimate, which the customer can approve or decline. This keeps the customer involved before paid technical work begins.

Third, the technician records the work performed and any parts used, then confirms the final bill. Finally, the system records payments and generates receipts. Once the balance is settled, the device can be released for pickup.

Each stage has a clear owner and a controlled status transition. That matters because the system is tracking more than a ticket. It is preserving the handoff, customer decision, technical work, billing, and final collection of the device.”

Transition: “The next slide focuses on the capacity rule that makes assignment reliable.”

## Slide 6 — Technician availability

“Technician availability combines two pieces of information: the technician’s duty status and the technical workload already assigned to that technician.

The system allows one active technical repair per technician. A technician marked Busy, Unavailable, or Off Duty cannot receive another assignment. This prevents the Admin from assigning work based only on a static name or calendar entry.

When the technician finishes the technical work, the active slot is released. Payment and pickup then continue as separate steps, so a repair that is awaiting payment does not unnecessarily block the technician from receiving another technical job.

This gives the shop a simple operational rule: assignment follows actual technical capacity, while payment and pickup remain part of the customer-service workflow.”

Closing line: “Together, these three slides show how CRSM turns a customer request into a traceable, capacity-aware repair process.”
